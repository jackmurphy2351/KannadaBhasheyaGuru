# Greetings and Introductions — ಶುಭಾಶಯಗಳು ಮತ್ತು ಪರಿಚಯ (*śubhāśayagaḷu mattu paricaya*)

Your first Kannada conversations will almost always follow the same short script: greet,
ask how someone is, trade names, say where you live and what you do, and take your leave
politely. Learn these as **whole phrases** first; the grammar behind them is covered in
the other lessons, linked below.

---

## 1. Saying Hello

| Kannada | Meaning | When |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ (*namaskāra*) | Hello, greetings | Anyone, any time. Always safe. |
| ನಮಸ್ತೆ (*namaste*) | Hello | Also fine; slightly less common in Karnataka. |
| ಹಲೋ (*halō*) | Hello | Casual; the usual way to answer the phone. |
| ಹಾಯ್ (*hāy*) | Hi | Young people, among friends. |

Add ಸಾರ್ (*sār*) or ಮೇಡಂ (*mēḍaṃ*) to make it polite to a stranger:

* ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ ಸಾರ್. (*Namaskāra sār.*) — Hello, sir.

> **Note:** There is no "good morning" in everyday speech; ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ (*namaskāra*) covers
> every time of day. ಶುಭೋದಯ (*śubhōdaya*), "good morning", and
> ಶುಭ ರಾತ್ರಿ (*śubha rātri*), "good night", exist, but they belong to greeting cards and
> text messages.

## 2. Respectful vs. Familiar "You"

Kannada has two words for *you*, and choosing the right one matters more than perfect
grammar:

* **ನೀವು (*nīvu*)**: respectful, and also the plural "you (all)". Use it with adults,
  elders, strangers, shopkeepers and colleagues. **When in doubt, use ನೀವು (*nīvu*).**
* **ನೀನು (*nīnu*)**: familiar. Only for children, close friends and younger siblings.

Every form of the pronoun follows the same split:

| | Respectful | Familiar |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| you | ನೀವು (*nīvu*) | ನೀನು (*nīnu*) |
| your | ನಿಮ್ಮ (*nimma*) | ನಿನ್ನ (*ninna*) |
| to you | ನಿಮಗೆ (*nimage*) | ನಿನಗೆ (*ninage*) |
| verb ending | -ಈರಿ (*-īri*): ಮಾಡ್ತೀರಿ (*māḍtīri*) | -ಈಯ (*-īya*): ಮಾಡ್ತೀಯ (*māḍtīya*) |

* ನಿಮ್ಮ ಊರು ಯಾವುದು? (*Nimma ūru yāvudu?*) — Which is your home town?
* ನಿನ್ನ ಶಾಲೆ ಎಲ್ಲಿದೆ? (*Ninna śāle ellide?*) — Where is your school? (to a child)

For the full verb endings, see *Verb Basics*.

## 3. "How Are You?" and Answers

* ಹೇಗಿದ್ದೀರಿ? (*Hēgiddīri?*) — How are you? (respectful)
* ಹೇಗಿದ್ದೀಯ? (*Hēgiddīya?*) — How are you? (familiar)

In speech the respectful question often ends in -ಆ (*-ā*): ಹೇಗಿದ್ದೀರಾ? (*Hēgiddīrā?*)

| Answer | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದ್ದೀನಿ (*cennāgiddīni*) | I'm well. (Formal: ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದ್ದೇನೆ (*cennāgiddēne*)) |
| ಪರವಾಗಿಲ್ಲ (*paravāgilla*) | Not bad. |
| ಸುಮಾರಾಗಿದ್ದೀನಿ (*sumārāgiddīni*) | So-so. |
| ಹುಷಾರಿಲ್ಲ (*huṣārilla*) | I'm not well (unwell). |

To return the question, add ನೀವು? (*nīvu?*), "and you?":

* ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದ್ದೀನಿ, ನೀವು? (*Cennāgiddīni, nīvu?*) — I'm well, and you?

### "What's new?"

* ಏನು ಸಮಾಚಾರ? (*Ēnu samācāra?*) — What's new? How are things? (spoken: ಏನ್ ಸಮಾಚಾರ?
  (*Ēn samācāra?*))
* ಏನು ವಿಶೇಷ? (*Ēnu viśēṣa?*) — Anything special?
* ಏನೂ ವಿಶೇಷ ಇಲ್ಲ. (*Ēnū viśēṣa illa.*) — Nothing much.

### "Have you eaten?"

A very common Karnataka greeting is to ask whether someone has eaten:

* ತಿಂಡಿ ಆಯ್ತಾ? (*Tiṇḍi āytā?*) — Have you had breakfast (or a snack)?
* ಊಟ ಆಯ್ತಾ? (*Ūṭa āytā?*) — Have you had lunch (or dinner)?
* ಹೂಂ, ಆಯ್ತು. ನಿಮ್ದು? (*Hūṃ, āytu. Nimdu?*) — Yes, I have. And you?

*Lit.:* Has breakfast happened? It is not an invitation to eat; it simply means "how's
your day going?" Formal: ತಿಂಡಿ ಆಯಿತಾ? (*Tiṇḍi āyitā?*)

## 4. Names

### Asking

* ನಿಮ್ಮ ಹೆಸರು ಏನು? (*Nimma hesaru ēnu?*) — What is your name? (spoken: ನಿಮ್ ಹೆಸರೇನು?
  (*Nim hesarēnu?*))
* ನಿನ್ನ ಹೆಸರು ಏನು? (*Ninna hesaru ēnu?*) — What's your name? (to a child)

### Answering: no word for "is"

Kannada needs **no verb at all** where English says *is* between two nouns:

* ನನ್ನ ಹೆಸರು ಮೀನಾ. (*Nanna hesaru Mīnā.*) — My name is Meena. *Lit.:* My name Meena.

Speakers often add **ಅಂತ (*anta*)**, "called", the same word that marks reported speech
(see *Reported Speech*):

* ನನ್ನ ಹೆಸರು ಮೀನಾ ಅಂತ. (*Nanna hesaru Mīnā anta.*) — My name is Meena. *Lit.:* My name (is)
  called Meena.

### Introducing someone

* ಇವರು ನನ್ನ ಸ್ನೇಹಿತರು, ಸುರೇಶ್. (*Ivaru nanna snēhitaru, Surēś.*) — This is my friend,
  Suresh.
* ಇವನು ನನ್ನ ತಮ್ಮ. (*Ivanu nanna tamma.*) — This is my younger brother.

ಇವರು (*ivaru*), "this person", is respectful; use it for any adult. ಇವನು (*ivanu*), "he",
and ಇವಳು (*ivaḷu*), "she", are for children and people younger than you. Family words are
in *Family and Home*.

### "Nice to meet you"

* ನಿಮ್ಮನ್ನ ನೋಡಿ ತುಂಬಾ ಖುಷಿ ಆಯ್ತು. (*Nimmanna nōḍi tumbā khuṣi āytu.*) — Very nice to meet
  you. *Lit.:* Having seen you, (there) was great happiness.

## 5. Where You Live, Where You Are From, What You Do

### Where you live

* ನೀವು ಎಲ್ಲಿ ಇರೋದು? (*Nīvu elli irōdu?*) — Where do you live?
* ನಾನು ಮಲ್ಲೇಶ್ವರದಲ್ಲಿ ಇದ್ದೀನಿ. (*Nānu Mallēśvaradalli iddīni.*) — I live in
  Malleshwaram.

*Lit.:* I am in Malleshwaram. Kannada uses ಇರು (*iru*), "be", for where you live.
-ಅಲ್ಲಿ (*-alli*) means *in / at* (see *Case Suffixes*).

### Where you are from

* ನಿಮ್ಮ ಊರು ಯಾವುದು? (*Nimma ūru yāvudu?*) — Which is your home town?
* ನೀವು ಯಾವ ದೇಶದವರು? (*Nīvu yāva dēśadavaru?*) — Which country are you from?
* ನಾನು ಅಮೆರಿಕದವನು / ಅಮೆರಿಕದವಳು. (*Nānu Amerikadavanu / Amerikadavaḷu.*) — I am from
  America (man / woman).
* ನಾನು ಮೈಸೂರಿನವಳು. (*Nānu Maisūrinavaḷu.*) — I'm from Mysuru (woman).

Place + -ಅವನು / -ಅವಳು / -ಅವರು (*-avanu / -avaḷu / -avaru*) means "a man / woman / person
of that place".

> **Note:** ಊರು (*ūru*) is your **native place**, where your family comes from, not the
> city you live in now. Someone born in Mysuru who has lived in Bengaluru for twenty years
> still answers ನನ್ನ ಊರು ಮೈಸೂರು (*nanna ūru Maisūru*).

### What you do

* ನೀವು ಏನು ಕೆಲಸ ಮಾಡ್ತೀರಿ? (*Nīvu ēnu kelasa māḍtīri?*) — What work do you do?
* ನಾನು ಒಂದು ಕಂಪನಿಯಲ್ಲಿ ಕೆಲಸ ಮಾಡ್ತೀನಿ. (*Nānu ondu kampaniyalli kelasa māḍtīni.*) — I
  work at a company.
* ನಾನು ಟೀಚರ್. (*Nānu ṭīcar.*) — I'm a teacher.
* ನಾನು ಓದ್ತಾ ಇದ್ದೀನಿ. (*Nānu ōdtā iddīni.*) — I'm a student. *Lit.:* "I am studying."

As with names, a job needs no "am": ನಾನು ಟೀಚರ್ (*nānu ṭīcar*), "I (am a) teacher".

## 6. Forms of Address

Using a bare first name for an older person is rude. Use one of these instead:

| Address | Literally | Use it for |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ಸಾರ್ (*sār*) | sir | Men in offices and shops; teachers; auto drivers. |
| ಮೇಡಂ (*mēḍaṃ*) | madam | Women in the same situations. |
| ಅಣ್ಣ (*aṇṇa*) | elder brother | A friendly way to address a man your age or a little older: a shopkeeper, a driver. |
| ಅಕ್ಕ (*akka*) | elder sister | The same, for a woman. |
| ಅಮ್ಮ (*amma*) | mother | An older woman. |
| name + ಅವರೇ (*avarē*) | "O respected …" | Calling a colleague or acquaintance politely: ಸುರೇಶ್ ಅವರೇ (*Surēś avarē*). |
| name + ಸಾರ್ / ಮೇಡಂ (*sār / mēḍaṃ*) | | At work: ಸುರೇಶ್ ಸಾರ್ (*Surēś sār*). |

When you talk **about** someone you respect, follow the name with ಅವರು (*avaru*) and use
the plural verb:

* ಸುರೇಶ್ ಅವರು ಬಂದ್ರು. (*Surēś avaru bandru.*) — Suresh has come. *Lit.:* Suresh they came.

## 7. Thanks, Sorry, and "No Problem"

| Kannada | Meaning | Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ಧನ್ಯವಾದ (*dhanyavāda*) | Thank you. | |
| ಧನ್ಯವಾದಗಳು (*dhanyavādagaḷu*) | Thank you (very much). | The plural is more formal. |
| ಥ್ಯಾಂಕ್ಸ್ (*thyāṅks*) | Thanks. | Very common in everyday speech. |
| ಕ್ಷಮಿಸಿ (*kṣamisi*) | Sorry. Excuse me. | Respectful; familiar ಕ್ಷಮಿಸು (*kṣamisu*). |
| ಸಾರಿ (*sāri*) | Sorry. | Everyday speech. |
| ಪರವಾಗಿಲ್ಲ (*paravāgilla*) | No problem. It's all right. | The reply to both thanks and sorry. |

> **Note:** Kannada speakers say "thank you" less often than English speakers for small
> everyday services, and among family and close friends a formal ಧನ್ಯವಾದ (*dhanyavāda*)
> can even sound distant. A smile, or ಸರಿ (*sari*), "OK", is often enough.

## 8. Survival Sentences for Learners

* ನಾನು ಕನ್ನಡ ಕಲೀತಾ ಇದ್ದೀನಿ. (*Nānu Kannaḍa kalītā iddīni.*) — I'm learning Kannada.
  For the formal form, see *Present Continuous*.
* ನನಗೆ ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ ಕನ್ನಡ ಬರುತ್ತೆ. (*Nanage svalpa svalpa Kannaḍa barutte.*) — I know a
  little Kannada.
  *Lit.:* A little Kannada comes to me. A language *comes to* you, so the speaker is
  in the dative ನನಗೆ (*nanage*), "to me" (see *Dative Constructions and Modals*).
* ನಿಮಗೆ ಇಂಗ್ಲಿಷ್ ಬರುತ್ತಾ? (*Nimage iṅgliṣ baruttā?*) — Do you speak English?
* ನಿಧಾನವಾಗಿ ಹೇಳಿ. (*Nidhānavāgi hēḷi.*) — Please say it slowly.

For "I don't understand" and "say that again", see *Everyday Phrases*.

## 9. Saying Goodbye

Kannada speakers avoid a bare "I'm going", because it sounds final. The person leaving
promises to come back, and the person staying sends them off with the same idea.

| Who says it | Kannada | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| The one leaving | ಹೋಗಿ ಬರ್ತೀನಿ (*hōgi bartīni*) | I'll be off. *Lit.:* I'll go and come. |
| The one leaving (short) | ಬರ್ತೀನಿ (*bartīni*) | I'll be off. *Lit.:* I'll come. |
| A group leaving | ಹೋಗಿ ಬರ್ತೀವಿ (*hōgi bartīvi*) | We'll be off. |
| The one staying | ಹೋಗಿ ಬನ್ನಿ (*hōgi banni*) | Goodbye. *Lit.:* Go and come (back). |
| The one staying (familiar) | ಹೋಗಿ ಬಾ (*hōgi bā*) | Bye. |
| Either | ಸಿಗೋಣ (*sigōṇa*) | See you. *Lit.:* Let's meet. |
| Either | ಆಮೇಲೆ ಸಿಗೋಣ (*āmēle sigōṇa*) | See you later. |
| Either | ನಾಳೆ ಸಿಗೋಣ (*nāḷe sigōṇa*) | See you tomorrow. |

Formal: ಹೋಗಿ ಬರುತ್ತೇನೆ (*hōgi baruttēne*).

On the phone, people end with:

* ಸರಿ, ಇಡ್ತೀನಿ. (*Sari, iḍtīni.*) — OK, bye. *Lit.:* OK, I'll put (the phone) down.

## 10. A Sample Conversation

* **A:** ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ! ಹೇಗಿದ್ದೀರಿ? (*Namaskāra! Hēgiddīri?*) — Hello! How are you?
* **B:** ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದ್ದೀನಿ, ನೀವು? (*Cennāgiddīni, nīvu?*) — I'm well, and you?
* **A:** ನಾನೂ ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದ್ದೀನಿ. ನಿಮ್ಮ ಹೆಸರು ಏನು? (*Nānū cennāgiddīni. Nimma hesaru ēnu?*) — I'm
  well too. What's your name?
* **B:** ನನ್ನ ಹೆಸರು ಅನಿತಾ. ನೀವು ಯಾವ ದೇಶದವರು? (*Nanna hesaru Anitā. Nīvu yāva dēśadavaru?*)
  — My name is Anita. Which country are you from?
* **A:** ನಾನು ಇಂಗ್ಲೆಂಡಿನವನು. ನಾನು ಕನ್ನಡ ಕಲೀತಾ ಇದ್ದೀನಿ. (*Nānu Iṅgleṇḍinavanu. Nānu Kannaḍa
  kalītā iddīni.*) — I'm from England. I'm learning Kannada.
* **B:** ಓಹೋ, ತುಂಬಾ ಒಳ್ಳೇದು! (*Ōhō, tumbā oḷḷēdu!*) — Oh, that's very good!
* **A:** ಸರಿ, ಹೋಗಿ ಬರ್ತೀನಿ. (*Sari, hōgi bartīni.*) — OK, I'll be off.
* **B:** ಹೋಗಿ ಬನ್ನಿ. ಸಿಗೋಣ! (*Hōgi banni. Sigōṇa!*) — Goodbye. See you!

## 11. Common Mistakes

* ❌ ನೀನು ಹೇಗಿದ್ದೀಯ? (*Nīnu hēgiddīya?*) to a shopkeeper or an elder.
  ✅ ನೀವು ಹೇಗಿದ್ದೀರಿ? (*Nīvu hēgiddīri?*) The familiar form to an adult you don't know is
  rude.
* ❌ ನನ್ನ ಹೆಸರು ಮೀನಾ ಇದೆ. (*Nanna hesaru Mīnā ide.*)
  ✅ ನನ್ನ ಹೆಸರು ಮೀನಾ. (*Nanna hesaru Mīnā.*) "X is Y" between two nouns takes no verb.
* ❌ ನಾನು ಕನ್ನಡ ಗೊತ್ತು. (*Nānu Kannaḍa gottu.*)
  ✅ ನನಗೆ ಕನ್ನಡ ಬರುತ್ತೆ. (*Nanage Kannaḍa barutte.*) Knowing a language takes the
  dative ನನಗೆ (*nanage*) and ಬರು (*baru*), "come".
* ❌ ನಾನು ಹೋಗ್ತೀನಿ. (*Nānu hōgtīni.*) as a goodbye.
  ✅ ಹೋಗಿ ಬರ್ತೀನಿ. (*Hōgi bartīni.*)
* ❌ Replying ಹೋಗಿ ಬರ್ತೀನಿ (*hōgi bartīni*) to someone who is leaving.
  ✅ ಹೋಗಿ ಬನ್ನಿ. (*Hōgi banni.*) The leaver says "I'll come"; the one staying says
  "come (back)".
* ❌ Calling an older colleague by their bare first name.
  ✅ ಸುರೇಶ್ ಅವರೇ (*Surēś avarē*) or ಸುರೇಶ್ ಸಾರ್ (*Surēś sār*).
* ❌ Taking ಊಟ ಆಯ್ತಾ? (*Ūṭa āytā?*) as an invitation to lunch. It is a greeting.

## For Hindi Speakers

* ನೀವು / ನೀನು (*nīvu / nīnu*) work like *āp / tum*. Kannada has no separate *tū* level;
  ನೀನು (*nīnu*) covers both *tum* and *tū*.
* ನನಗೆ ಕನ್ನಡ ಬರುತ್ತೆ (*nanage Kannaḍa barutte*) is built exactly like *mujhe Kannaḍ ātī
  hai*: dative speaker, and the verb "come".
* Name + ಅವರೇ (*avarē*) or ಅವರು (*avaru*) does the job of name + *jī*.
* ಧನ್ಯವಾದ (*dhanyavāda*) is the same Sanskrit word as *dhanyavād*.
* ಹೋಗಿ ಬರ್ತೀನಿ (*hōgi bartīni*) is like *acchā, caltā hū̃*, but it promises a return.
