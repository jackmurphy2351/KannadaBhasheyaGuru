# Sentence Structure and Word Order — ವಾಕ್ಯ ರಚನೆ (*vākya racane*)

This is the **first thing** an English speaker has to rewire. English builds sentences as
**Subject–Verb–Object**. Kannada builds them as **Subject–Object–Verb**: the verb comes
**last**. The same "last" rule runs through the whole language: describing words come
before what they describe, and the main clause comes after everything that leads up to it.

---

## 1. The Core Difference: SVO vs. SOV

| Language | Order | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| English | Subject → **Verb** → Object | I **eat** rice. |
| Kannada | Subject → Object → **Verb** | I rice **eat**: ನಾನು ಅನ್ನ ತಿಂತೀನಿ. (*Nānu anna tintīni.*) |

* ನಾನು ಅನ್ನ ತಿಂತೀನಿ. (*Nānu anna tintīni.*) — I eat rice. ನಾನು (*nānu*) "I" + ಅನ್ನ (*anna*)
  "rice" + ತಿಂತೀನಿ (*tintīni*) "eat".
* ಅವಳು ಪುಸ್ತಕ ಓದ್ತಾಳೆ. (*Avaḷu pustaka ōdtāḷe.*) — She reads a book.

Train your ear to **wait for the verb**: it lands at the very end.

---

## 2. The "Last" Rule Everywhere

Kannada is consistently **head-final**: whatever is described or depended on comes last.
Very often the Kannada order is the English order **reversed**.

| English | Kannada | Literally |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| a **big** house | ದೊಡ್ಡ ಮನೆ (*doḍḍa mane*) | big house |
| Rama's house | ರಾಮನ ಮನೆ (*Rāmana mane*) | Rama's house |
| the man **who came** | ಬಂದ ಮನುಷ್ಯ (*banda manuṣya*) | came man |
| **on** the table | ಮೇಜಿನ ಮೇಲೆ (*mējina mēle*) | table's on |
| I **want to go** | ನನಗೆ ಹೋಗಬೇಕು (*nanage hōgabēku*) | to-me go-want |
| **if** it rains, … | ಮಳೆ ಬಂದ್ರೆ… (*maḷe bandre…*) | rain if-comes, … |
| I know **that** he came | ಅವನು ಬಂದ ಅಂತ ನನಗೆ ಗೊತ್ತು (*avanu banda anta nanage gottu*) | he came that to-me known |

So: adjectives, possessors and relative clauses go **before** the noun; postpositions and
case endings go **after** it; the main verb comes before its helpers; and "if", "when"
and "because" clauses come **before** the main clause.

---

## 3. A Safe Default Order

When in doubt, use:

> **(Time) → Subject → (Place) → (To whom) → Object → (How) → Verb**

| Time | Subject | Place | To whom | Object | Verb |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ನಾಳೆ (*nāḷe*) — tomorrow | ನಾನು (*nānu*) — I | ಆಫೀಸಲ್ಲಿ (*āphīsalli*) — at the office | ಅವನಿಗೆ (*avanige*) — to him | ಪುಸ್ತಕ (*pustaka*) — the book | ಕೊಡ್ತೀನಿ (*koḍtīni*) — will give |

ನಾಳೆ ನಾನು ಆಫೀಸಲ್ಲಿ ಅವನಿಗೆ ಪುಸ್ತಕ ಕೊಡ್ತೀನಿ. (*Nāḷe nānu āphīsalli avanige pustaka koḍtīni.*) —
I'll give him the book at the office tomorrow.

* The person who receives (dative) usually comes **before** the thing given:
  ನಾನು ಅಮ್ಮನಿಗೆ ಹೂವು ಕೊಟ್ಟೆ. (*Nānu ammanige hūvu koṭṭe.*) — I gave Mum flowers.
* Adverbs of manner go just before the verb: ಅವನು ನಿಧಾನವಾಗಿ ಮಾತಾಡ್ತಾನೆ. (*Avanu nidhānavāgi
  mātāḍtāne.*) — He speaks slowly.

---

## 4. Why Word Order Is Flexible

In English, word order **is** the grammar: "Dog bites man" and "Man bites dog" mean
opposite things. In Kannada the **case endings** say who does what (see *Case Endings vs.
Prepositions*), so the other words can move without changing the meaning:

* ನಾಯಿ ಮನುಷ್ಯನನ್ನ ಕಚ್ಚಿತು. (*Nāyi manuṣyananna kaccitu.*) — The dog bit the man.
* ಮನುಷ್ಯನನ್ನ ನಾಯಿ ಕಚ್ಚಿತು. (*Manuṣyananna nāyi kaccitu.*) — The dog bit the man (it was the
  man the dog bit).

Moving a word changes the **emphasis**, not the meaning. The word just before the verb
usually carries the focus:

* ನಾನು ನಿನ್ನೆ ಮಾರ್ಕೆಟ್‌ಗೆ ಹೋದೆ. (*Nānu ninne mārkeṭge hōde.*) — I went to the market yesterday.
* ನಿನ್ನೆ ನಾನು ಮಾರ್ಕೆಟ್‌ಗೆ ಹೋದೆ. (*Ninne nānu mārkeṭge hōde.*) — *Yesterday*, I went to the market.

### The Verb Can Come Early in Speech
Ending with the verb is the safe default, and always correct in writing. In relaxed
speech, people often say the verb first and add the rest as an afterthought:

* ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದೆ ಈ ಸಿನಿಮಾ. (*Cennāgide ī sinimā.*) — It's good, this film.
* ಬಂದ್ರಾ ಅವರು? (*Bandrā avaru?*) — Have they come?

---

## 5. Leaving Words Out

The verb ending already says who is acting, so the subject is often dropped. Words that
are clear from context can go too:

* ಎಲ್ಲಿಗೆ ಹೋಗ್ತಾ ಇದ್ದೀರಾ? (*Ellige hōgtā iddīrā?*) — Where are you going?
* ಆಫೀಸಿಗೆ. (*Āphīsige.*) — To the office.
* ಕಾಫಿ ಕುಡಿದ್ರಾ? (*Kāphi kuḍidrā?*) — Have you had coffee?
* ಕುಡಿದೆ. (*Kuḍide.*) — I have. *Lit.:* (I) drank (it).

---

## 6. Sentences Without a Verb, and "To Me" Sentences

* **"X is a Y" has no verb at all:** ಇದು ನನ್ನ ಕಾರು. (*Idu nanna kāru.*) — This is my car.
  ಅವರು ನಮ್ಮ ಟೀಚರ್. (*Avaru namma ṭīcar.*) — She is our teacher. See *Iru vs. Āgu*.
* **"To me" sentences** put the person first, in the dative: ನನಗೆ ಕನ್ನಡ ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ ಬರುತ್ತೆ.
  (*Nanage Kannaḍa svalpa barutte.*) — I know a little Kannada. See *Dative Constructions and Modals*.

---

## 7. Negatives and Questions Keep the Same Order

* **Negatives** go at the very end, where the verb is: ನಾನು ನಾಳೆ ಬರಲ್ಲ. (*Nānu nāḷe baralla.*) —
  I won't come tomorrow. ಇದು ನನ್ನ ಕಾರು ಅಲ್ಲ. (*Idu nanna kāru alla.*) — This isn't my car.
* **Yes/no questions** don't swap words around. Add **-ಆ** (*-ā*) to the verb:
  ನೀವು ಊಟ ಮಾಡಿದ್ರಾ? (*Nīvu ūṭa māḍidrā?*) — Have you eaten?
* **Question words** stay where the answer would go; they don't move to the front:
  ನೀವು ಎಲ್ಲಿಗೆ ಹೋಗ್ತಾ ಇದ್ದೀರಿ? (*Nīvu ellige hōgtā iddīri?*) — Where are you going? (Answer:
  ನಾನು ಆಫೀಸಿಗೆ ಹೋಗ್ತಾ ಇದ್ದೀನಿ. (*Nānu āphīsige hōgtā iddīni.*))
* **-ಆ** (*-ā*) **on another word** questions that word: ನೀನಾ ಮಾಡಿದ್ದು? (*Nīnā māḍiddu?*) —
  Was it *you* who did it?
* **Tags** go at the end: ಇವತ್ತು ಭಾನುವಾರ, ಅಲ್ವಾ? (*Ivattu bhānuvāra, alvā?*) — Today's
  Sunday, isn't it?

---

## 8. Emphasis: -ಏ (*-ē*) and "It Was X That…"

* **-ಏ** (*-ē*) **marks "this one and no other"**: ನಾನೇ ಮಾಡಿದೆ. (*Nānē māḍide.*) — I did it
  myself. ಇವತ್ತೇ ಬನ್ನಿ. (*Ivattē banni.*) — Come today itself.
* **"It was X that…"**: put the verb in the "thing" form (-ದ್ದು (*-ddu*); see *Relative
  Clauses*) and end with the focused word: ನಾನು ಬಂದಿದ್ದು ನಿನ್ನೆ. (*Nānu bandiddu ninne.*) —
  It was yesterday that I came.

---

## 9. Longer Sentences: Lead-Up First, Main Clause Last

Clauses of time, reason, condition and sequence end in a linking form and come **first**.
The main clause comes last:

* ಮಳೆ ಬಂದಿದ್ದಕ್ಕೆ ನಾವು ಹೊರಗೆ ಹೋಗಲಿಲ್ಲ. (*Maḷe bandiddakke nāvu horage hōgalilla.*) — We
  didn't go out because it rained.
* ಮನೆಗೆ ಹೋಗಿ, ಸ್ನಾನ ಮಾಡಿ, ಮಲಗಿದೆ. (*Manege hōgi, snāna māḍi, malagide.*) — I went home, had a
  bath and went to bed.

See *Conjunctions*, §3, and *Conditional*.

## 10. Practice Patterns

| English | Literal Kannada order | Kannada |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| I drink water. | I water drink. | ನಾನು ನೀರು ಕುಡಿತೀನಿ. (*Nānu nīru kuḍitīni.*) |
| He goes home. | He home-to goes. | ಅವನು ಮನೆಗೆ ಹೋಗ್ತಾನೆ. (*Avanu manege hōgtāne.*) |
| We'll watch a film tomorrow. | Tomorrow we film will-watch. | ನಾಳೆ ನಾವು ಸಿನಿಮಾ ನೋಡ್ತೀವಿ. (*Nāḷe nāvu sinimā nōḍtīvi.*) |
| She gave her mother a book. | She her mother-to book gave. | ಅವಳು ಅವಳ ಅಮ್ಮನಿಗೆ ಪುಸ್ತಕ ಕೊಟ್ಟಳು. (*Avaḷu avaḷa ammanige pustaka koṭṭaḷu.*) |
| The book I bought yesterday is on the table. | Yesterday bought book table's on is. | ನಿನ್ನೆ ತಗೊಂಡ ಪುಸ್ತಕ ಮೇಜಿನ ಮೇಲಿದೆ. (*Ninne tagoṇḍa pustaka mējina mēlide.*) |

## 11. Common Mistakes

* ❌ ನಾನು ತಿಂತೀನಿ ಅನ್ನ (*nānu tintīni anna*) (English order) → ✅ ನಾನು ಅನ್ನ ತಿಂತೀನಿ (*nānu anna
  tintīni*). Put the verb last.
* ❌ ಮನುಷ್ಯ ಬಂದ (*manuṣya banda*) for "the man who came" → ✅ ಬಂದ ಮನುಷ್ಯ (*banda manuṣya*).
  The description goes before the noun.
* Question words don't need to move to the front as in English:
  ✅ ನೀವು ಎಲ್ಲಿಗೆ ಹೋಗ್ತಾ ಇದ್ದೀರಿ? (*Nīvu ellige hōgtā iddīri?*) The order ಎಲ್ಲಿಗೆ ನೀವು… (*ellige nīvu…*) is heard
  too, but it isn't required.
* ❌ ನಾನು ಡಾಕ್ಟರ್ ಇದ್ದೀನಿ (*nānu ḍākṭar iddīni*) → ✅ ನಾನು ಡಾಕ್ಟರ್ (*nānu ḍākṭar*). "X is a Y"
  needs no verb.

---

## 12. Quick Reference

* **Verb last**, negatives and question endings with it.
* **Describers before** the noun: adjectives, possessors, relative clauses.
* **Endings and postpositions after** the noun.
* **Lead-up clauses first**, main clause last.
* **Case endings, not position,** show who does what, so the order can move for emphasis.
* **Drop** whatever context already makes clear.

---

## For Hindi Speakers

* Hindi is also SOV with postpositions, so most of this will feel familiar.
* Two differences: Hindi relative clauses often follow the noun (*vo ādmī jo āyā*), but
  Kannada puts them before it (ಬಂದ ಮನುಷ್ಯ (*banda manuṣya*)). Hindi *ki*-clauses follow the
  verb (*maĩ jāntā hūṁ ki…*), but Kannada puts them before it with ಅಂತ (*anta*) (… ಅಂತ ಗೊತ್ತು
  (*anta gottu*)).
