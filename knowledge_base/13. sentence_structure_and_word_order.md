
# A Beginner's Guide to Sentence Structure & Word Order (ವಾಕ್ಯ ರಚನೆ)

This is the **first thing** an English speaker must rewire. English builds
sentences as **Subject–Verb–Object (SVO)**. Kannada builds them as
**Subject–Object–Verb (SOV)** — the verb almost always comes **last**.

## 1. The Core Difference: SVO vs. SOV

| Language | Order | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **English** | Subject → **Verb** → Object | I **eat** rice. |
| **Kannada** | Subject → Object → **Verb** | I rice **eat**. (*Nānu anna tinnuttēne.*) |

So the literal word-by-word reading of a Kannada sentence sounds like
"**I rice eat**", "**I shop-to go**", "**She book reads**". Train your ear to
**wait for the verb** — it lands at the very end.

* ನಾನು ಅನ್ನ ತಿನ್ನುತ್ತೇನೆ. (*Nānu anna tinnuttēne.*) — I eat rice.
  * *Nānu* (I) + *anna* (rice) + *tinnuttēne* (eat).
* ಅವಳು ಪುಸ್ತಕ ಓದುತ್ತಾಳೆ. (*Avaḷu pustaka ōduttāḷe.*) — She reads a book.
  * *Avaḷu* (She) + *pustaka* (book) + *ōduttāḷe* (reads).

---

## 2. Why Word Order Feels "Free" (But the Verb Doesn't Move)

Here is the magic: in English, word order **is** the grammar. "Dog bites man"
and "Man bites dog" mean opposite things. In Kannada, the **case suffix**
(see *Case Endings vs. Postpositions*) tells you who does what — so the
subject and object can move around for emphasis **without changing the meaning**.

* ನಾನು ನಿನ್ನೆ ಮಾರ್ಕೆಟ್‌ಗೆ ಹೋದೆ. (*Nānu ninne mārketge hōde.*) — I went to the market yesterday.
* ನಿನ್ನೆ ನಾನು ಮಾರ್ಕೆಟ್‌ಗೆ ಹೋದೆ. (*Ninne nānu mārketge hōde.*) — *Yesterday* I went to the market. (same meaning, slight emphasis shift)

**The one rule that does NOT bend:** the **verb stays at the end**. You can
shuffle the other pieces, but finish with the verb.

---

## 3. Default Slot Order (The Safe Template)

When in doubt, follow this beginner-safe order:

> **(Time) → Subject → (Place) → Object → Verb**

| Time | Subject | Place | Object | Verb |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ನಾಳೆ (*Nāḷe*, Tomorrow) | ನಾನು (*Nānu*, I) | ಅಂಗಡಿಯಲ್ಲಿ (*aṅgaḍiyalli*, in the shop) | ಹಾಲು (*hālu*, milk) | ಕೊಳ್ಳುತ್ತೇನೆ (*koḷḷuttēne*, will buy) |

ನಾಳೆ ನಾನು ಅಂಗಡಿಯಲ್ಲಿ ಹಾಲು ಕೊಳ್ಳುತ್ತೇನೆ.
(*Nāḷe nānu aṅgaḍiyalli hālu koḷḷuttēne.*) — Tomorrow I will buy milk in the shop.

---

## 4. Modifiers Come BEFORE the Word They Describe

Adjectives, possessives, and numbers all sit **in front of** the noun, just
like English — this part is friendly.

* ದೊಡ್ಡ ಮನೆ (*doḍḍa mane*) — big house
* ನನ್ನ ಪುಸ್ತಕ (*nanna pustaka*) — my book
* ಎರಡು ಬಾಳೆಹಣ್ಣು (*eraḍu bāḷehaṇṇu*) — two bananas

Relative/descriptive clauses also come **before** the noun (the opposite of
English "the man **who came**"). That is an advanced topic — for now, just
remember: **describers point forward to the noun**, and the **verb waits at the end**.

---

## 5. Questions Keep the Same Order

You do **not** flip the sentence to ask a question (no "Do you...?" inversion).
You either add a question word or the particle **-ā / -ē**.

* ನೀವು ಊಟ ಮಾಡಿದಿರಾ? (*Nīvu ūṭa māḍidirā?*) — Did you eat? (statement order + *-ā*)
* ನೀವು ಎಲ್ಲಿಗೆ ಹೋಗ್ತೀರಿ? (*Nīvu ellige hōgtīri?*) — Where are you going? (question word *ellige* = "to where", verb still last)

---

## 6. Quick Practice Patterns

| English (SVO) | Literal Kannada (SOV) | Kannada |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| I drink water. | I water drink. | ನಾನು ನೀರು ಕುಡಿಯುತ್ತೇನೆ. (*Nānu nīru kuḍiyuttēne.*) |
| He goes home. | He home-to goes. | ಅವನು ಮನೆಗೆ ಹೋಗುತ್ತಾನೆ. (*Avanu manege hōguttāne.*) |
| We see a film. | We film see. | ನಾವು ಸಿನಿಮಾ ನೋಡುತ್ತೇವೆ. (*Nāvu sinimā nōḍuttēve.*) |

**Takeaway:** Think the English thought, then **move the verb to the end** and
attach the right suffix to the object/place. That single habit unlocks Kannada
sentence-building.
