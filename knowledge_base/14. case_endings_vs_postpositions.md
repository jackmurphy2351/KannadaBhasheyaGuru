
# A Beginner's Guide to Case Endings vs. Prepositions (ವಿಭಕ್ತಿ ಮತ್ತು ಪರಸರ್ಗ)

In English, little words come **before** the noun: *to* the shop, *in* the box,
*with* a pen, *from* America. These are **prepositions** (they sit in *pre*
position). Kannada has almost none of these. Instead, the meaning gets **glued
onto the end of the noun** as a **case suffix**. The direction flips: English
puts the word **before**, Kannada attaches it **after**.

## 1. The Big Idea: English Word → Kannada Suffix

| English (preposition before) | Kannada (suffix after) | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **to / for** the house | mane**ge** | ಮನೆಗೆ (*manege*) — to the house |
| **in / at** the shop | aṅgaḍi**yalli** | ಅಂಗಡಿಯಲ್ಲಿ (*aṅgaḍiyalli*) — in the shop |
| **from / by / with** the bus | bassi**ninda** | ಬಸ್ಸಿನಿಂದ (*bassininda*) — from the bus |
| **of / 's** Rama | Rāma**na** | ರಾಮನ (*Rāmana*) — Rama's |

Notice there is **no separate word**. *To the house* is one word: *manege*.
This is why you must learn the **seven case suffixes** (covered fully in
*Case Suffixes / Vibhakti*). Here is the beginner cheat-sheet:

| English idea | Suffix | Mnemonic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| to / for | **-ge / -kke / -ige** | "give it **-ge** to me" |
| from / by / with (means) | **-inda** | "came-**inda** America" |
| in / at | **-alli** | "**alli** = there/at" |
| of / 's | **-a** | possessive **-a** |
| (object marker) | **-annu / -nna** | marks the thing acted on |

## 2. Side-by-Side: Same Sentence, Two Languages

> **English:** I am going **to** the market **with** my friend.

* Word-for-word it has TWO prepositions: *to* and *with*.
* Kannada turns both into suffixes and sends the verb to the end:

ನಾನು ನನ್ನ ಸ್ನೇಹಿತನ ಜೊತೆ ಮಾರ್ಕೆಟ್‌ಗೆ ಹೋಗ್ತೀನಿ.
(*Nānu nanna snēhitana joteyalli mārketge hōgtīni.*)

* *mārket* + **ge** = **to** the market
* *joteyalli* / *jote* = **with / together** (a postposition word — see §4)

---

## 3. "Postpositions": The Few Helper Words That DO Exist

Kannada is not 100% suffixes. A handful of **postpositions** behave like English
prepositions but come **after** the noun, and they usually attach to a noun
**already in a case form** (often genitive *-a* or dative *-ge*).

| Postposition | Meaning | Attaches to | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ಮೇಲೆ (*mēle*) | on / above | genitive (*-a*) | ಮೇಜಿನ ಮೇಲೆ (*mējina mēle*) — on the table |
| ಕೆಳಗೆ (*keḷage*) | under / below | genitive (*-a*) | ಮರದ ಕೆಳಗೆ (*marada keḷage*) — under the tree |
| ಒಳಗೆ (*oḷage*) | inside | genitive (*-a*) | ಮನೆಯ ಒಳಗೆ (*maneya oḷage*) — inside the house |
| ಜೊತೆ (*jote*) | with (together) | genitive (*-a*) | ಅವನ ಜೊತೆ (*avana jote*) — with him |
| ಹತ್ತಿರ (*hattira*) | near | genitive (*-a*) | ಬಸ್ ಸ್ಟಾಪ್ ಹತ್ತಿರ (*bas sṭāp hattira*) — near the bus stop |
| ಮುಂದೆ (*munde*) | in front of | genitive (*-a*) | ಬಾಗಿಲ ಮುಂದೆ (*bāgila munde*) — in front of the door |

**Rule of thumb:** *direction/location/source/possession* = **suffix** glued on.
*Spatial relationships* (on, under, near, in front of, with) = a **separate
postposition word** sitting after a genitive noun.

## 4. Common Beginner Mistakes

1. **Don't translate the preposition as its own word for the core cases.**
   "to the house" is NOT *"ge mane"* and NOT *"mane ge"* (two words) — it is
   **manege** (one word). ❌ *naanu ge mane hogteeni* → ✅ ನಾನು ಮನೆಗೆ ಹೋಗ್ತೀನಿ.
2. **Don't forget the oblique stem.** Before a suffix, many nouns change slightly
   (*mara* + *alli* → *mara**d**alli*, "in the tree"). See *Case Suffixes*.
3. **English "with" splits into two ideas in Kannada:**
   * *with a pen* (instrument) → **-inda**: ಪೆನ್ನಿನಿಂದ (*penninda*).
   * *with my friend* (company) → **jote / joteyalli**: ಸ್ನೇಹಿತನ ಜೊತೆ.

## 5. Practice: Spot the Suffix

| English | Kannada | Glued-on piece |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| I came from Mysore. | ನಾನು ಮೈಸೂರಿನಿಂದ ಬಂದೆ. (*Nānu Maisūrininda bande.*) | **-inda** = from |
| The book is on the table. | ಪುಸ್ತಕ ಮೇಜಿನ ಮೇಲೆ ಇದೆ. (*Pustaka mējina mēle ide.*) | **mēle** = on |
| Give it to the child. | ಮಗುವಿಗೆ ಕೊಡು. (*Maguvige koḍu.*) | **-ge** = to |
| This is my mother's house. | ಇದು ನನ್ನ ಅಮ್ಮನ ಮನೆ. (*Idu nanna ammana mane.*) | **-a** = 's / of |

**Takeaway:** Stop looking for a word to translate "to / in / from / of." Ask
instead: *which suffix do I glue onto the noun?* That mindset shift is the whole
game for English speakers learning Kannada.
